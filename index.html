MODEL & METHODOLOGY

Designed to be explained.

Design and Simulation of an IoT-Driven Smart Traffic Light Control System: A Case Study of Ojuelegba, Lagos.

How the controller decides

Read queue counts each simulated second → classify demand → allocate green at the next phase → update signal → record metrics → move vehicles → repeat. North → East → South → West rotates without skipping, preventing starvation. Fixed mode gives every approach 25 seconds. Responsive mode gives low queues 15 seconds, medium 25 seconds and high 40 seconds. Each phase ends with 3 seconds amber and 2 seconds all-red clearance. Only the green approach discharges vehicles.

Assumptions and academic limitations

Arrivals follow seeded Poisson demand in vehicles per minute. Each approach has one idealised incoming lane; one vehicle is served every 2 seconds during green. Detection is perfect, transmission is instantaneous and lossless, and the cloud is an in-browser module. No turns, pedestrians, lane changing, incidents, or real road geometry are modelled. These are illustrative inputs, not field measurements. Responsive control is not guaranteed to outperform fixed timing in every scenario. Use several seeds and calibrated demand for a research evaluation.

Metrics and signal timing

Waiting time is time spent in the queue. Flow is discharged vehicles divided by elapsed simulated minutes. Average queue is the sum of queue observations divided by elapsed seconds; maximum and final queues are also recorded. Fixed cycle = 120 seconds including clearance. Responsive cycles range from 80 to 180 seconds. Average cycle includes only completed cycles; green averages include allocated phases. Per-approach red time is elapsed time since that approach was last green.
